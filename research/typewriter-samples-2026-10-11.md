# Typewriter samples that are free to use, surveyed

Answers *"You don't have to implement anything, but do a research of
typewriter samples, which are free to use"*, asked 2026-10-11 for the `typist`
concepts group (`BACKLOG.md`, top entry). Two pages need it: **010** plays a
typewriter on every key, and **040** gives every participant a DIFFERENT
typewriter. The wish is public domain and, ideally, multisampled: letter keys
in several variants, space, backspace, carriage return, the bell, shift.

🔴 **NOTHING WAS DOWNLOADED AND NOTHING WAS HEARD.** Every size, rate and
duration below is read off the host's own listing page. No archive was fetched,
no file was decoded, and nothing here says how anything sounds except where the
uploader said it.

## The verdict

**THERE IS NO FREE, PUBLIC DOMAIN, FULLY MULTISAMPLED TYPEWRITER.** The two
sets shaped exactly like a sampler wants are not public domain:

- Subtersonic's **Brother Charger 11** pack (press and release per key, space,
  backspace, shift, new line, ding) says *"If you want to redistribute them,
  please CONTACT ME FIRST"*. Serving its files from positron.studio is
  redistribution.
- soundslikewillem's **A to Z, one file per letter** is **CC BY-NC 4.0**.

What does exist under CC0 is enough, though, because a typewriter has few
distinct sounds: a type bar strike, the space bar, the bell, the carriage
return, the shift, and the paper knob. Letters differ from each other by
little on a real machine, so a few strike variants plus small WebAudio pitch
and gain jitter is a faithful sampler, not a fake one.

**010, one typewriter: BigSoundBank's Hermes Precisa 305, all CC0.** It is the
only CC0 source with a single key, the space bar and two bells cut as separate
files, from one machine, on one professional chain (Sound Devices MixPre-3 and
a Neumann KM184, 48 kHz, 24 bit). Seven longer takes of the same machine (8 s to
88 s) are where further letter variants and a carriage return get cut from.
There is no isolated backspace or shift for it anywhere; backspace is a key
strike pitched down, shift is a key strike with a slower, heavier envelope.
If no clean carriage return is in those takes (UNCONFIRMED until listened to),
knufds' *Typewriter bell and carriage reset* (CC0, antique machine) fills it.

**040, a different typewriter per participant: four CC0 machines whose
characters differ by design, not by EQ.**

| seat | machine | why it is different | isolated files on offer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hermes Precisa 305 (BigSoundBank) | manual portable, studio clean | key, space, bell x2, seven takes to cut |
| 2 | "Silver Typewriter" (moodyfingers, Freesound) | manual, phone mic, denoised | keystroke, space, shift x2, carriage return lever x3, margin release, margin release with return |
| 3 | IBM Selectric II (secretmojo, Freesound) | ELECTRIC golfball office machine, motor hum | none, one 2:03 take to cut; its bell is broken |
| 4 | Barbie e115c toy electronic typewriter (sprinkleCipher, Freesound) | plastic toy, electronic, whirring return | key x3, space, carriage return x3, paper knob, power switch |

More machines, all CC0 and all needing cutting out of a long take, if the
room grows past four: a 1946 L C Smith (ColinMWJones), an Olivetti Lettera 22
(keithpeter), a Hermes Ambassador (jeromeblum), a 1960s Smith-Corona Corsair
(BennettFilmTeacher, and megashroom's at 96 kHz), a 1920s Remington (rpnelson),
a 1970s Olimpia (ikomlino, recorded on a Sennheiser 416).

Freesound uploaders ASK for one and say it is optional. A credit line on the
page costs nothing and is what was asked for.

⚠️ **FREESOUND NEEDS A LOGIN TO DOWNLOAD.** DOCUMENTED on the pack page:

## What could not be settled

1. **How any of it sounds.** Nothing was downloaded or heard. The pick for 010
   rests on the recording chain on the page, not on listening. The first build
   step is listening to the Hermes key against the Silver keystroke.
2. **Whether the Hermes long takes hold a clean carriage return.** The pages
   say *"I write slowly on the typewriter"*. A line end with bell and return
   is likely in an 88 s take and is not promised anywhere.
3. **How much cutting the long takes need.** Seat 3 and the extra machines
   are one long file each. Slicing is an hour per machine at a guess, and the
   strikes may overlap at speed (UNCONFIRMED).
4. **The BBC RemArc wording at source.** The licence page is a JavaScript app
   that refused the fetch; the quote is from its front page and the press.
5. **Whether Subtersonic would say yes.** The blog's contact route is from
   2012. Its Brother Charger 11 is the only multisample with press and
   release per key type, so it is the one worth one email if the CC0
   material sounds thin.
6. **yottasounds keys 1 and 2 being identical**, per a comment, unchecked.

## What it would cost

The 010 set as mp3 from BigSoundBank, as listed: key 26 KB, space 24 KB, bell
61 KB, plus a few cut variants and a return, about **150 to 250 KB** for one
machine (the variants are an estimate). Four machines for 040 at that
density, **under 1 MB**, copied once into our storage and served from the
site like `/nola/`'s piano. Nothing opened to anybody else's server per visit.
