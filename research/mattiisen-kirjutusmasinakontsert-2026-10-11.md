## What the piece is

- DOCUMENTED. Title *Kirjutusmasina kontsert D-duur*, 1984, 19 minutes, four movements:
  I Allegro vivace e marcato, II Largo molto tranquillo, III Allegro agitato, IV Finale.
  Scored for flute, guitar, bass guitar, drums, vibraphone, xylophone, typewriter,
  synthesizers and electronics. Premiered 1984 by the ensemble In Spe at the VI Tartu
  Levimuusikapäevad (Tartu popular music days). Source: Estonian Music Information Centre
  works list, https://emic.ee/?action=view&id=57&lang=est&method=teosed&mid=32&sisu=heliloojad
- DOCUMENTED. It is the work that first made Mattiisen known: *"Esmase tuntuse tõid talle
  aga Tartu levimuusikapäevad, eriti suurvorm 'Kirjutusmasina kontsert D-duur' (1984)
  ansambli 'In Spe' esituses."* Source: https://bettimuuseum.org/alo-mattiisen/ (the same
  sentence is in the emic.ee biography,
  https://www.emic.ee/?sisu=heliloojad&mid=32&id=57&lang=est&action=view&method=biograafia).
- DOCUMENTED. It is a concerto in the literal sense: the typewriter is the solo
  instrument and In Spe is the orchestra. Recalled in a local history piece (Helve Laasik,
  Jõgevamaa, 17 April 2026): *"Kontsert on see, kus on soolopill ja orkester. Orkester oli
  InSpe. Soolopill oli kirjutusmasin ja me teame, mida nõukogude ajal kirjutusmasinaga teha
  sai. Paberi peale sai kõike kirja panna ja see oli ka protest ühiskonna vastu."*
  https://www.jogevamaa.info/post/j%C3%B5geva-linna-lood-tegelikult-on-alo-mattiiseni-loomingus-h%C3%A4sti-palju-p%C3%B5nevat-kuulake
  UNCONFIRMED: the reading of the typewriter as a protest symbol is this author's
  interpretation in 2026, not anything attributed to Mattiisen in 1984.
- DOCUMENTED. Context: Mattiisen (22 April 1961 Jõgeva to 30 May 1996 Tallinn) took over
  In Spe from Erkki-Sven Tüür in 1983, first concert with them January 1984, as keyboard
  player and composer. Best known for the *Viis isamaalist laulu* (Five patriotic songs),
  premiered Tartu 14 May 1988, among them *Ei ole üksi ükski maa*, which became Singing
  Revolution staples. The typewriter concerto is four years earlier and belongs to his
  symphonic prog rock period, not the patriotic song period.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alo_Mattiisen
- DOCUMENTED. It is the only work in Mattiisen's emic.ee catalogue with a typewriter in it.

## Relation to Leroy Anderson's *The Typewriter* (1950)

- UNCONFIRMED either way. No source read connects the two. They are different kinds of
  piece: Anderson's is a roughly two minute orchestral novelty with a typewriter soloist
  (key clatter, the bell, the carriage return as a slide whistle like gesture); Mattiisen's
  is an 18 to 19 minute four movement concerto for a rock band with synthesizers, in a
  symphonic prog idiom. The title shape ("concerto", a key, classical movement names)
  points to the classical concerto form, played straight with some irony, rather than to
  Anderson. One English review quoted in a search summary reads it as *"an ironic
  orchestra confronts a clumsy typewriter eager to become a musical instrument"* (via
  ProgArchives, https://www.progarchives.com/album.asp?id=595, which answered 403 to a
  direct read, so UNCONFIRMED as quoted). Another review there calls the typewriter
  "silly" in retrospect (same caveat).

Who played the typewriter: UNCONFIRMED. A search summary of the Discogs Musea CD
credits gives *"Percussion [Typewriter] – I.Ivarts (tracks: 1 to 4)"*, that is drummer
Ivo Varts (1961 to 2013), with a note that the booklet wrongly gives track 1 only. Discogs
refused a direct read. One other source places Varts in In Spe around 1987 to 1990, which
does not fit a 1984 or 1985 recording, so this credit needs checking against the LP
sleeve or the ESTER record's performer notes. The 1984 Tartu performer of the typewriter
part is not named anywhere read.

Who wrote it: emic.ee and the ESTER record credit Mattiisen as composer (the LP is
"художественный руководитель Ало Маттийсен"). One search summary of an archive entry
said "composed by In Spe"; UNCONFIRMED and likely a metadata shortcut.

## Score

- UNCONFIRMED: no published score was found. The emic.ee works entry gives instrumentation,
  duration and premiere but no publisher or sheet music listing, and no edition turned up
  in ESTER. For a rock band piece from 1984 the parts most likely exist only as manuscript
  or band parts, possibly with the family or the Estonian Music Information Centre
  (emic.ee, which keeps manuscript copies of many Estonian works). The route to find out is
  to ask EMIC directly.
- Nothing was found about how the typewriter part is notated (whether keys, bell and
  carriage return are written as separate percussion lines, as Anderson does).

## Rights position

- Mattiisen died 30 May 1996. Estonian copyright (Autoriõiguse seadus) runs for the
  author's life plus 70 years, counted from 1 January after the death, so the composition
  is protected until the end of **2066**. Rights are administered for Estonian composers
  by EAÜ (Eesti Autorite Ühing) and held by the heirs.
- The recordings carry their own related rights on top of that: the 1985 Melodiya master
  (performers and producer rights; under the EU's 70 year term for phonograms published
  after 1963 that would run to about 2055, UNCONFIRMED how it applies to a Soviet master),
  the 1994 Musea reissue, and the 2023 digital release which states ℗ 2023 Alo Mattiisen.
- What that means for a public positron page: a recording, an excerpt, a sample cut from
  it, or a transcription or reproduction of the score cannot be put on the site without
  permission from the rights holders. A description in our own words, the facts above,
  short quotation for comment, and links out to the catalogue and streaming pages are
  fine. An embed of somebody else's player would also open someone else's server on visit,
  which this project's rules already forbid unless pressed.

## What it could mean for the typist pages (ideas only, nothing built)

- A heritage anchor: an Estonian piece from 1984 in which a typewriter is the soloist and a
  band accompanies it is the same shape as `typist` 040, where several typewriters type
  together. A one line mention with a link to the emic.ee entry (not a player) could sit on
  the group's index card or a page's comment, the way the radio pages point at ERR.
- The concerto form as a page idea: one participant is "the soloist" whose typing is the
  lead voice, the others accompany (quieter, a different machine, or tied to a beat).
  That is a role in the room, not a sound, and it fits the patchbay vocabulary (one link
  louder than the rest).
- The four movements suggest tempo characters a page could offer as sample texts in 020's
  choice group: a fast marcato text, a slow tranquil text, an agitated one, a short finale.
- The Laasik reading (in Soviet times a typewriter could write anything on paper) is a
  usable framing for a multi person typing page, said as hers, not as Mattiisen's.
- The musical gestures any typewriter piece uses, Anderson's or Mattiisen's, are the ones
  the samples research already lists: key strike, space, bell, carriage return. Nothing
  read here says how Mattiisen wrote them, so do not claim it.
- Nothing from the recording may be sampled. The CC0 machines in
  `research/typewriter-samples-2026-10-11.md` remain the sound source.

### The answer: the manuscripts are at the Theatre and Music Museum

DOCUMENTED. The emic.ee works list carries a "Käsikiri" link under the entry, which
opens EMIC's manuscript register:
https://www.emic.ee/index.php?sisu=kasikirjad&mid=229&lang=est&action=view&id=13319
(read 2026-10-11). Title there: *Kirjutusmasinakontsert D-duur*, genre LEVIMUUSIKA,
4 movements, 1984. It lists five items, all with location **Eesti Teatri- ja
Muusikamuuseum** (TMM):

| type | kind | pages | status | sketch | TMM number | note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Autorikäsikiri (autograph) | Partituur (full score) | 52 | Lõpetatud (finished) | | ETMM_10510 M 441:2/23 | |
| Valguskoopia (blueprint or photocopy) | Partituur | 98 | Lõpetatud | | ETMM_11841 M 441:2/30 | |
| Autorikäsikiri | Instrumentaalpartiid (parts) | 8 | Lõpetatud | Jah | ETMM_11841 M 441:2/30 | **Kirjutusmasinapartii** (the typewriter part) |
| Autorikäsikiri | Klaviir (piano reduction) | 28 | Lõpetatud | Jah | ETMM_11841 M 441:2/30 | |
| Autorikäsikiri | Partituur | 20 | | Jah | ETMM_11841 M 441:2/30 | 1. ja 2. osa (movements 1 and 2) |

So a finished autograph full score exists, a 98 page copy of a score exists, and an
8 page autograph typewriter part exists. That last one is the document that would say
how Mattiisen notated the typewriter (keys, bell, carriage return). Nothing about its
content is known yet.

UNCONFIRMED: whether "M 441" is a Mattiisen personal fonds at TMM, and whether any of
these pages are among the roughly 4,000 TMM manuscripts digitised into MuIS in the
2020 project (ERR, 16 Dec 2020,
https://kultuur.err.ee/1209109/teatri-ja-muusikamuuseum-digiteerib-eesti-heliloojate-4000-originaalkasikirja
names other composers, not Mattiisen). A plain fetch of the MuIS search
(https://www.muis.ee/search?q=Kirjutusmasinakontsert) returned only the empty search
form, so this needs a person in a browser, or the question to the museum.
