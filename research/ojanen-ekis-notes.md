# Ojanen's EKIS thesis: what is in it, and what this project can use (2026-09-15)

Analysis of Mikko Ojanen, *User Stories of Erkki Kurenniemi's Electronic Musical
Instruments, 1961-1978* (PhD, University of Helsinki, 2020). The extracted text
is `research/ojanen-ekis-thesis.md`, with its provenance, licence and page map
in that file's header. **320 PDF pages, xxvi + 293 printed pages, CC-BY-4.0 on
the text.** Every page number below is the **printed** one.

Companion documents, read before this one:
`research/kurenniemi-sources-2026-09.md` and
`research/kurenniemi-archive-2026-08.md`. Section 6 says exactly where this
thesis confirms, contradicts or extends them.

**Every claim is labelled.** `READ` = it is in the document, page given.
`MEASURED` = a system was asked and this is what it answered, with the time.

## 1. The diary question, answered first

**The thesis uses the diaries as a primary source, cites individual cassettes by
archive number, dates entries to the day, and says where they are held. It does
not quote them at length, does not say they are digitised, and does not say they
are public.**

That is the short answer. The detail:

### 1.1 What the diaries are

READ, p. 13:

> "Kurenniemi constantly archived his life with non-stop note-making,
> photographing and recording. This activity produced an extensive collection of
> diaries in various formats such as handwritten notebooks and hours of audio
> and video recordings. One unique source that is of relevance here is his
> collection of taped audio diaries (1970-1975), which consists of over 100
> hours of his talk, usually when driving alone in a car."

(The two dashes inside that sentence in the original are en dashes and are
rendered here as commas. The exact original wording is on p. 13 of the extract.)

READ, p. 14, continuing directly from the above:

> "For example, when driving from Helsinki to Tampere on Sunday October 8, 1972,
> Kurenniemi describes his whereabouts and surroundings at a given moment, and
> he reads the register plates of other cars aloud. Most interestingly, he
> reminisces on what he did recently, where he is coming from, where he is going
> to, and why. This extraordinary collection of living historical source
> material has vast research potential."

So there are at least **four distinct diary formats** named in the thesis, and
they are not interchangeable:

| form | span given | where cited |
|---|---|---|
| **taped audio diaries** (c-cassette) | **1970-1975**, over **100 hours** | p. 13, and by cassette id throughout |
| **handwritten notebooks** | not dated as a series | p. 13 |
| **video recordings** | not dated as a series | p. 13 |
| **daily planners** (pocket diaries) | **1968-1975** | p. 13 |
| **DIMI-päiväkirja** (a project diary) | **1971-1972** | p. 146 n. 318, p. 258 |

⚠️ READ, p. 13: the daily planners are described as part of the **University
Studio (UHMRL) archive collection**, not the National Gallery one. The audio
diaries are in the National Gallery one. **The diaries are split across two
institutions**, which matters for anyone asking a custodian for them.

### 1.2 The cassettes it cites by number

READ, p. 258 (the unpublished archive material list, under "The Finnish National
Gallery / Erkki Kurenniemi archive"):

> "Kurenniemi's audio diaries C4000, C4060, C4113, C4136, C4137, C4138."

READ, p. 145 n. 316 cites a seventh, **C4059**, which is absent from that
bibliography list. INFERRED: the bibliography list is incomplete rather than
C4059 being something else, because it is cited in the same form as the others
and in the same archive. Flagged rather than asserted.

Dated to the day, all READ:

| cassette | date | what the thesis says it contains | page |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C4000 / R-4021** | **December 18-20, 1971** | "Kurenniemi's **first** audio tape diary c-cassette". Notes on a trip to Peter Zinovieff's studio in London to present the DIMI-A | p. 136 and n. 288 |
| **C4060 / R-4086** | **October 8, 1972** | Labelled `'72-10-08 Dimi demo TRD dimensio + "Sunday 12:31"`. Driving from the Vironkatu studio, having met Ruohomäki who was maintaining the DIMIX; picked up the DIMI-O en route to the Dimensio exhibition in Tampere | p. 106 n. 179 |
| **C4059** | not dated in the text | Cited for the Oulu Symphony / DIMI-O concert of October 18, 1972 | p. 145 n. 316 |
| **C4113** | not dated in the text | Cited for DIMI-T use, near a studio course session of December 10, 1973 | p. 150 n. 330 |
| **C4136, C4137, C4138** | not dated in the text | Cited three times for the September 1971 DIMI-O / Yle demonstration and the "Dimi ballet" | p. 143 n. 312, p. 144 n. 313 |

🔴 **The C4000 date carries a contradiction I could not resolve, and I am not
resolving it by picking.** READ, p. 136: the body text places the London visit
"In December", in a paragraph whose preceding dated event is **November 17,
1970**; footnote 288 says the cassette recording it is dated **December 18-20,
1971**. Those are a year apart. Either the trip was December 1970 and the diary
note about it was made a year later, or the body's "In December" means December
1971. **The thesis does not say which**, and the difference matters because it is
the difference between the DIMI-A being marketed abroad before or after the
January 1971 Digelius launch. INFERRED, and weakly: "A few notes about the trip
**survive on**" reads more like a later recollection than a same-week entry, but
that is a reading of one preposition and should not be used as a date.

### 1.3 What the diaries are used to establish

Each of these is a claim the thesis rests on a diary, all READ:

- **July 22, 1968** (p. 128): "According to Kurenniemi's **daily diaries**,
  Lindeman met Kurenniemi on July 22, 1968", placed just as Sähkökvartetti was
  being finished before its Bulgaria premiere.
- **January 21, 1971** (p. 141): "According to Kurenniemi's **diary entries**, he
  came up with the suffix O for the next DIMI on January 21, 1971 after having
  purchased the camera and monitor." The same passage dates the prototype as
  "still in its infancy on February 15", and "on May 10 it was in working
  condition".
- **September 22, 1971** (p. 144 n. 313): a **daily planner** entry quoted
  verbatim in full, and it is three words: `12.15 "Studio 4"`.
- **early 1972** (p. 150): "Kurenniemi's **diary entries** reveal that he drafted
  the MCS-8s (Micro-Computer Set by Intel) in early 1972", sourced in n. 335 to
  "Kurenniemi's **DIMI diary** in The Finnish National Gallery collections".
- **September 1971** (p. 144): "Later, in his **diary notes**, Kurenniemi
  referred to the demonstration session as somewhat successful", about the Yle
  DIMI-O session.
- **1970s, undated** (p. 249): "explaining in his **diary** that he was starting
  to use the artist role to facilitate or accelerate the marketing of his
  instrument."

### 1.4 What the thesis says about the diaries as objects

READ, p. 228, the only characterisation of their content as a whole:

> "As revealed in his diaries, his train of thought switched seamlessly from one
> topic to another. The resemblance to Thomas Edison's notebooks as Hughes
> (1986, 285) describes them is evident. [...] He mixed this with girlfriends,
> sex, pornography, and narcotics, even on the most concrete material level of
> taping pubic hair on his diary pages beside the technical drawings (see
> Paasonen 2013, 55)."

READ, p. 60: the diaries are named as one of the cross-checking sources against
interviews: "I crosschecked the interview material with other sources such as
contemporary newspaper articles, journals, diaries, and daily planners".

### 1.5 Held where, and are they digitised or public

- **Held**: READ, p. 258 and the abbreviation list p. vii. The audio diaries and
  the DIMI-päiväkirja are in **FNG/EKA, the Erkki Kurenniemi Archive at the
  Finnish National Gallery**. The daily planners are in the **UHMRL archive** at
  the University of Helsinki (p. 13, p. 259).
- **Deposited**: READ, p. xx (preface): "Mainly due to Rastas's efforts,
  Kurenniemi's private collection was deposited in the FNG in **2006**, where his
  legacy remains **at the disposal of researchers**."
- **Digitised?** **The thesis does not say the diaries are digitised.** READ,
  p. 15, what Ojanen digitised is listed explicitly and the audio diaries are not
  in the list: "digital repro-photographed tape reels and their covers,
  (hand)written documents (letters, notebooks, memos etc.), pictures and
  scrapbooks". INFERRED: "notebooks" there could include diary notebooks, but the
  sentence does not say so and I will not read it as saying so.
- **Public?** **No.** They are listed under "Archive material (**unpublished**)"
  (p. 258). READ, p. 17, the governing policy, which is the closest the thesis
  comes to answering this: "In such cases, I keep material containing sensitive,
  confidential or copyrighted information secure, and **only open the metadata**."
  INFERRED, and the §1.4 content makes it strong: audio diaries mixing business
  with sex, drugs and named living people are exactly the "sensitive" category
  that clause exists for. **Expect metadata, not content.**

### 1.6 What the thesis does NOT do with them

Stated plainly so nobody assembles something adjacent:

- **It never quotes a sentence of an audio diary.** Every audio-diary citation is
  a paraphrase plus a cassette number. The only diary text quoted verbatim in the
  whole thesis is a three-word daily-planner entry (p. 144 n. 313).
- **It gives no inventory.** No count of cassettes, no list of shelf marks beyond
  the seven ids, no date range per cassette. "Over 100 hours" (p. 13) is the only
  quantity.
- **It gives no access procedure**, no reading-room terms, no licence for the
  diaries, and no catalogue reference to look one up by.
- **It says nothing about the video diaries** beyond naming them once (p. 13).

## 2. What EKIS is, and the instruments

**EKIS is not a thing in the world. It is an acronym the research group coined
for its own subject and then used to brand its datasets and a website.** READ,
p. 11 n. 6: it expands to **"Erkki Kurenniemi's Electronic Musical Instruments"**.

It attaches to three things:

1. **The thesis title** (the Zenodo filename is `..._User_Stories_of_EKIS_...`).
2. **Two Zenodo datasets** named for it: the *User Interface and Functionality
   Charts of EKIS* (p. 11 n. 6, p. 156 n. 351) and the *Analyses of the works of
   art and events related to EKIS* (p. 11 n. 6).
3. **A website, `http://ekis.helsinki.fi`**, cited three times (pp. 5, 156, 275)
   as where "the descriptions and material are presented" (p. 156).

🔴 **MEASURED 2026-09-15T12:00Z: that website does not exist as documentation.**
`http://ekis.helsinki.fi` answers `200` and redirects to
`https://blogs.helsinki.fi/electronic-musical-instruments-by-kurenniemi/`, a
WordPress with **6 pages and 1 post**. The page titled *Custom built instruments*
(id 104) has a body of exactly one word, **"Lorum"**. *Kurenniemi's instruments in
the 2000s* (id 99) is **empty**. The single post, dated **2018-11-18**, is titled
*"Publishing of EKIS.HELSINKI.FI postponed"* and says publication "needs to be
postponed [...] We will set a new publishing date in the near future."
**Eight years on it is still a stub.** So the thesis's primary pointer for
instrument documentation resolves to nothing, and any plan that assumed
`ekis.helsinki.fi` is a data source is wrong. Only the *Introduction* page
(id 2) carries real prose.

Framing statements around it, all READ, p. 80:

- "Kurenniemi built electronic musical instruments and studio equipment between
  the years of **1964 and 1975**."
- Three project categories: the University Studio including the Integrated
  Synthesizer (1964); custom-designed instruments commissioned by artists
  (Sähkökvartetti, Andromatic, DICO); and the **Digelius period (1970-1975)**
  DIMI series.
- "His instruments have been **identified, their locations are known**, and most
  of them have been **restored and are in working condition**."

⚠️ **The instrument count is stated three different ways and they do not agree.**
READ: Table 3 has **13 rows** (11 built, 2 never built). p. 158 says "I
concentrate on his **nine** musical instruments and leave the studio equipment
aside". MEASURED on the EKIS site today, its *Introduction* page says "between
1964 and 1975 Kurenniemi built **ten** innovative instruments". INFERRED: nine =
13 minus DIMIX (studio equipment), minus DIMI-U and DIMI-P (never built), minus
DIMI-H (out of period), plus one; the arithmetic does not quite close and I did
not force it. **If this project ever prints a count, print Table 3's rows and
say which filter was applied**, because "how many instruments did he build" has
no single answer in the source.

Restoration history, READ, p. 80 n. 88: Sähkökvartetti and DIMI-O (Jari
Lehtinen, 2002), DICO (Lehtinen, 2003), DIMI-A and DIMI-O maintenance (Lehtinen,
2013-14), **"DIMI is reborn": replication of the DIMI-A** (Jari Suominen, 2014),
Integrated Synthesizer generator unit (Suominen, 2017-18).

### 2.2 Per-instrument detail worth knowing

READ, ch. 6.2.1, pp. 162-170. Condensed to what a page might actually say:

- **Integrated Synthesizer** (p. 162): five-step sequencer, oscillators, harmony
  generator, cluster generator of **ten VCOs**, summing mixer, five **10x10 patch
  matrixes**. Hybrid digital and analog. READ, p. 157: Suominen found that
  Kurenniemi's first known sequencer **"preceded Buchla's by several months"**.
- **Sähkökvartetti** (p. 162): mainframe plus **six mobile controllers**; electric
  drums, melody machine, electric saxophone, electric violin, singer's distortion
  chain, 10-step sequencer. ⚠️ **"the violin controller has vanished"**.
- **Andromatic** (pp. 162-163): 10-step polyphonic sequencer, one oscillator per
  step; with every flip-flop in counter mode it plays a **1024-step** sequence.
- **DICO** (p. 163): 12-step monophonic, two output channels, **ten bits per
  step** programmed by grounding pins (4 pitch, 3 octave, 2 articulation, 1
  channel).
- **DIMI-A** (pp. 164-165): two-voice, **256-step** sequencer, programmed by
  touching **2x16 metal plates with two styluses**. Memory holds **up to 100
  commands**. ⚠️ p. 165: the user manual "is typically in the form of a printed
  sheet of paper on top of the instrument".
- **DIMI-O** (p. 166): the video organ. Four-octave keyboard, video camera,
  monitor, **32-step sequencer with 48-note polyphony**, **1,536-bit memory**
  (32x48). Camera image thresholded to black and white triggers notes; in logical
  product mode it can "pick the sounds from the air". **Ideally two operators.**
- **DIMI-S** (p. 168): skin conductivity, four players forming **six pairs**, six
  voices. "The less clothing the players have on, the higher the number of
  connections."
- **DIMI-T** (p. 169): EEG from the scalp, **amplified 500,000 times**, controls
  one monophonic VCO. READ, and this is the thesis being careful: the claim that
  a player could learn to produce a recognisable melody "has neither been tested
  nor proved plausible".
- **DIMI 6000** (pp. 169-170): microcomputer-controlled analog synth, programmed
  in **octal on an ADDS Consul 880 terminal**, two **ITT SL 56** cassette mass
  storage units. Kurenniemi wrote **DISCORD**; Ruohomäki later wrote **DISMAL**
  and **DISEQ** "which overcame the problems in Kurenniemi's original DISCORD".

## 3. The method, and what its sources actually were

READ, ch. 1.2, pp. 11-17. It is a **multi-archive documentary history**, not an
oral-history project, though interviews are a large component.

### 3.1 The stance on sources

READ, p. 11, and it is unusually close to this project's own rules:

> "this study is largely based on non-catalogued, unorganized, fragmented
> documents and physical artifacts of various kinds. A certain unfinishedness,
> which is a distinctive feature of the source material, challenges the research
> and leaves a good deal of room for interpretation. For example, markings on the
> tape reels are sparse, the musical instruments lack a user manual and the
> technical specifications are defective, and most of the archive material is
> undocumented."

READ, p. 16, on why interviews are ranked below documents: "I consider
memory-based material a **secondary source** because it cannot be trusted without
cross-referencing."

READ, p. 12, the limits declared up front: users he could not interview, people
who died mid-project, **non-users** who tried the instruments and rejected them,
and "the skewed gender balance in the research material [...] The almost total
lack of women in the sources".

### 3.2 The sources, named

**Archives** (READ, p. 257, the full list, 22 entries). The load-bearing ones:

- **UHMRL**, the University of Helsinki Music Research Laboratory and Electronic
  Music Studio archive. READ, p. 13: "handwritten documentation about most of
  Kurenniemi's instruments, as well as the sound-recording archive with
  **approximately 350 reel-to-reel tapes**". Also holds the **daily planners
  (1968-1975)**, the **inventory catalog of studio equipment (1958-1974)**,
  purchase documents, funding applications, annual reports, and "a handwritten
  sound archive catalog (**initiated by Kurenniemi in 1972**)".
- **FNG/EKA**, the Erkki Kurenniemi archive at the Finnish National Gallery: the
  audio diaries, the DIMI diary, letters and correspondence, the written
  instructions for *Deal* (October 1971), the 1973 description of digital
  instruments, photographs.
- **Yle**, **Lehtikuva** (the picture agency), the **National Archive** (holding
  the Digelius Electronics Finland documents), the **National Library**, the
  **Theater Museum**, the **HYY Student Union archive**, the **Amos Anderson Art
  Museum**, the **Oulu Symphony Orchestra archive**, **Music Finland**.
- **Seven private archives**, named after their holders: Jouko Kottila, Jukka
  Ruohomäki, Leo Nilsson, Osmo Lindeman, Pekka Hoikkala, Pekka Sirén, Ralph
  Lundsten, Risto Rautee. READ, p. 14: "Some even entrusted original documents to
  us, most notably Lindeman's collection including his tape archive, other sound
  recordings, books, letters, sheet music, scores, and a scrapbook."

**Interviews.** READ, p. 16: "I started conducting open thematic interviews in
**2004**", with Jari Suominen, Kai Lassfolk and Marko Home. Archived as both
transcriptions (`.docx`) and audio (`.wav` and `.mp3`). READ, pp. 261-264: the
interview list runs four pages. A remark on p. 16 worth keeping, on why the audio
matters and not just the transcript:

> "in an audio recording it is possible to hear how the interviewer fills gaps in
> the interviewee's memory with almost silent gestures, something that cannot be
> perceived even from an accurate transcription."

**Tapes.** READ, p. 13: of the ~350 UHMRL reels, "The tapes related to his
instruments, which I have **digitized, restored and analyzed** to some extent
during this study, comprise approximately **20-30 percent** of the archive."
READ, p. 15: high-resolution WAV, annotated as spectrogram images in Steinberg
Wavelab, archived as `.wav`/`.mp3` plus `.tiff`/`.jpg` sonograms.

⚠️ READ, p. 15, a warning this project should take seriously because it applies
to every restored tape it might ever play: "The signal processing may result in
**different versions of a work** and thus could even influence the music
analysis. Therefore, the processing of the audio material should be considered in
the interpretation."

**Newspapers.** READ, p. 15: "Within only a few hours of browsing, I gathered
**200-300 contemporary newspaper articles**", from the National Library's digital
archive, Helsingin Sanomat and Dagens Nyheter chiefly.

### 3.3 The method's own output, and where it went

READ, pp. 15-17. Ojanen set up **a Zenodo community, `electronic-musical-
instruments-by-kurenniemi`**, precisely so the data would outlive the thesis, and
stated the licence policy: "The research material and data for which I have the
right of use, or the right to issue licenses to third parties, are published
under **Creative Commons Attribution (CC-BY)**." He also says the metadata schema
was built "to be **interoperable** with a standardized format in order to merge
the data sets with official databases such as **Finna.fi or Europeana** in the
future".

MEASURED 2026-09-15T12:02Z: that Zenodo community currently holds **33 records**
(`zenodo.org/api/records?communities=electronic-musical-instruments-by-kurenniemi`,
`hits.total` = 33). `kurenniemi-sources-2026-09.md` counted 28 from a
`q=Kurenniemi` keyword search, so **the community query finds five more records
than the keyword query does**.

A worked example of the method, and the best single illustration of why this
thesis is more useful than a memoir. READ, p. 96 n. 147: Ruutsalo's published
memoir describes producing sound material for *Hyppy* "in the Vironkatu studio",
and Ojanen notes in the footnote that **the department did not move to Vironkatu
until two and a half years later**. The memory is wrong and the annual report
catches it. READ, p. 96, the conclusion he draws rather than papering over:
**"The dates of these recording sessions are unknown."**

### 🔴 The On-Off case, which is the most interesting one on the page

This is worth more than a tightened date, because it is a source that **refuses
to tighten**. READ, pp. 88-89:

- The work premiered at the Jyväskylän Kesä festival on **July 11, 1963**,
  alongside Salmenhaara's *White Label*.
- But Kurenniemi, interviewed in 2004, associated the **recording session** with
  the studio's move to the Porthania cellar, "which could have happened at the
  **end of 1962**".
- "The container of the On-Off master tape **does not include any date
  markings**."
- The university's annual report for 1963-64 says the studio moved to the cellar
  "**in the fall of 1963**", which contradicts the memory.
- Ojanen's conclusion, verbatim: **"The exact dates of the move, and of
  Kurenniemi's On-Off session remain unknown."**

INFERRED: `/tapes/` currently shows On-Off at `year 1963` via
`wikidata-title-match`, and `kurenniemi-sources-2026-09.md` proposes moving it to
**January 1963** on the Appearances CSV's `19630100`. **The thesis is evidence
against narrowing it that far.** The honest reading is a band from late 1962 to
July 11 1963 with a hard upper bound at the premiere, carrying the disagreement
rather than resolving it. This is the one item in the corpus where a named
scholarly source argues for **more** uncertainty, not less, and that is exactly
the case the tratteggio design in `kurenniemi-archive-2026-08.md` §5b was built
to draw.

## 6. What this project could do with it, and what it could not

### 6.1 Against `kurenniemi-sources-2026-09.md` and `kurenniemi-archive-2026-08.md`

**CONFIRMS**

- That the **Appearances CSV is the right thing to build against**. The thesis
  cites it twice as the canonical event list (pp. 82, 211) and prints its own
  24-row summary as Table 7. The sources doc ranked it first; the thesis is the
  peer-reviewed argument for that ranking.
- That the **Zenodo corpus is CC-BY and author-curated**. READ, p. 17: Ojanen
  states the CC-BY policy himself and says why he set the community up.
- That **the institution holding the archive is the one you cannot reach**
  (archive doc, lesson 7). The thesis confirms the FNG deposit (2006, p. xx) and
  confirms the diaries and correspondence there are **unpublished** (p. 258).
- That **date precision must be declared rather than inferred**. Table 7's
  `[s.a. 1973?]` cells and "The exact dates [...] remain unknown" (p. 89) are the
  same discipline, in prose.
- The **National Library press archive** as a real source: Ojanen pulled 200-300
  articles from it (p. 15). The sources doc measured 1279 hits and found every one
  `authorized: false`. Both are true and they fit: he had terminal access, this
  host does not.

**EXTENDS**

- 🔴 **FinEARS and Kielipankki**, in neither document. A named, citable,
  URN-identified interview corpus, plus a project whose stated purpose is to open
  the digitised tape collection. This is the most valuable new lead in the thesis.
  Blocked today (403), but it is a **different door** from Finna and worth its own
  ask.
- **Table 3** gives an instrument chronology with **build years and quantities
  built**, including two never built. Neither existing document has per-instrument
  dates at all; the corpus carries three instrument records with institutional
  year ranges only.
- **Nine of the thirteen vague `/tapes/` items get a better date**, from a named
  scholar with an argument, not from a filename.
- 🔴 **The `Sähkösoittimen ääniä` re-attribution** (p. 205 n. 402) is the single
  most valuable line in the thesis for this project. Both tracks are outtakes from
  the *Vihreä eläin* radio play (1971), and the CD "credits [them] to Kurenniemi
  [...] **Vihreä eläin is not mentioned as the origin of the sound material**."
  That is a documented case of a commercial release stripping provenance, which is
  precisely the failure mode the corpus's `rightsAsserter` and
  `dateEvidence.how` fields exist to expose.
- **The tape count**: ~350 reels at UHMRL, 20-30% instrument-related and
  digitised. Neither document had a number for the physical holding.
- **The processing warning** (p. 15): restoration produces "different versions of
  a work". Nothing in either document accounts for the possibility that two
  digitisations of one tape are two different objects.

**CONTRADICTS**

- 🔴 **The proposed On-Off date.** `kurenniemi-sources-2026-09.md` §1.1 proposes
  moving On-Off from a ten-year band to **January 1963** on the CSV's `19630100`,
  scored as "10 yr to 1 month". **The thesis says the session date is unknown and
  may be late 1962** (p. 89), with a master tape carrying **no date markings**.
  Those are the same author. INFERRED: the CSV row records an *appearance* under a
  yyyymm00 convention, and the prose records a *recording session*, so they are
  not formally in conflict; but taking the CSV cell as a recording date produces a
  statement the author contradicts in prose, which is worse than the ten-year band
  it replaces.
- ⚠️ **`ekis.helsinki.fi` is not a source.** Nothing in either document claims it
  is, but the thesis points at it three times, so anyone reading the thesis would
  assume it. MEASURED today: a stub with a 2018 postponement notice.
- **Zenodo record count.** The sources doc says 28 records from `q=Kurenniemi`.
  MEASURED today, the community query returns **33**. Not a contradiction of the
  measurement, a contradiction of treating it as the whole corpus.

### 6.2 What to do, concretely

1. **Re-date the two `Sähkösoittimen ääniä` items to 1971 and record the
   *Vihreä eläin* origin**, with a new `dateEvidence.how` of
   `ojanen-thesis-p205-n402`. It is one edit, it moves two of the nine worst
   items, and it adds a provenance correction nothing else in the corpus carries.
2. **Add the other seven dates from §4**, each citing the printed page. Six move
   from a decade to a year or better.
3. **Leave `Virsi` and `Computer Music` exactly where they are**, and say in the
   page why: the thesis is silent on both. A source that covers the corpus
   thoroughly and does not mention an item is weak evidence about that item, and
   pretending otherwise would be the "partial result that is too tidy" failure.
4. **Make On-Off the page's worked example of disagreement.** Two named sources,
   one widening and one narrowing, with the master tape's blankness as the
   physical fact under both.
5. **Resolve the Appearances **concept** DOI**, `10.5281/zenodo.842854`, not the
   version. It is a moving dataset by the author's own description.
6. **Ask about FinEARS and Kielipankki** in any rights conversation, as a
   separate ask from Finna. The interview corpus already has a URN, which means
   somebody has already done the deposit work.

### 6.3 What it could not do, honestly

- **It ships no audio and no images this project can use.** The thesis is a PDF.
  The 350 tapes are not in it, the diaries are not in it, and the photographs in
  it are **excluded from the CC-BY licence** (p. ii, list on pp. viii-xi), with
  Lehtikuva and the FNG holding most of them. **Nothing visual from this document
  can go on a page.**
- **It does not unblock a single endpoint.** Finna, Yle and the FNG item-level
  finding aid are exactly as closed after reading it as before. The one new lead,
  Kielipankki, is **403 from here** (MEASURED).
- **It cannot date the tapes this project actually streams, as files.** Its dates
  are for *works*, *sessions* and *appearances*. The archive.org MP3s are
  transfers of a 2002 CD, and the gap between "the work was made in 1971" and
  "this file is a rip of a 2002 master of a 1971 session" is the exact trap
  `kurenniemi-archive-2026-08.md` lesson 1 is about. **The thesis sharpens the
  work date and says nothing about the file.**
- **It is a 2020 snapshot of a moving target.** The author says so himself
  (p. 12): "I anticipate and welcome future discoveries, which will eventually
  enhance my interpretation." The Zenodo datasets have been revised since; the
  thesis has not.
- **It does not resolve its own C4000 contradiction** (§1.2), and this project
  should not resolve it either.
