Five ancestors listened to keydown and rebuilt the cursor by hand; all five refused arrow keys, none read selectionStart even once, and the newest regressed to value += key. This one captures semantic ops — beforeinput/input resolved to {type, range, text} against the document — so reduce(prefix ≤ t) folds to {text, selection} and seek works. Arrow keys are not handled: they are structurally invisible to the capture and only move a selection that rides as its own op.

the two lanes are the proof

–

Lane A is built by actuate() applying one op at a time; lane B is folded independently by reduce() over the whole prefix. Nothing copies one into the other while the transport is running. If the reducer were wrong they would drift apart on screen. Samples within 40 wall-ms × rate of an op are skipped as settling — the exclusion window is dispatch jitter, a wall-clock quantity, so it must be converted into the position domain by the rate. Any remaining mismatch is classified: dispatch lag (lane A equals the fold one dispatch either side) or a real divergence — the only number that would mean the reducer is wrong.
